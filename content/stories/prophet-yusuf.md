## The dream

Yūsuf ﷺ was a boy when he told his father Yaʿqūb ﷺ what he had seen in his
sleep: eleven stars, and the sun and the moon, prostrating to him. His
father understood at once that Allah had chosen his son, and told him not to
repeat the dream to his brothers.

## The well

The brothers were jealous. They took Yūsuf ﷺ out with them, threw him into
a well, and returned to their father with his shirt and false blood on it,
saying a wolf had eaten him. Yaʿqūb ﷺ did not believe them, but he had no
proof, and he answered with beautiful patience.

A passing caravan drew Yūsuf ﷺ up from the well and sold him in Egypt for a
small price, as though he were worth nothing.

## The house, and the prison

He was bought into the house of a powerful man and grew up there. When he
was tempted and pressed, he refused, and he was imprisoned for a crime he
had not committed. He stayed in prison for years.

There he did what he had always done — he called the men in the cells with
him to worship Allah alone, and Allah gave him knowledge of the meaning of
dreams.

## The years of plenty, and the years of hunger

When the king of Egypt dreamt of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones,
nobody could explain it — until a man remembered the prisoner who interpreted
dreams. Yūsuf ﷺ told them plainly: seven years of plenty, then seven years
of famine. Store the grain in its ear, and eat sparingly from what you save.

He was brought out, cleared, and put in charge of the storehouses of the
land. The boy who had been sold for a small price now held the food of a
kingdom.

## The brothers, again

The famine came, and it reached his brothers. They travelled to Egypt for
grain and stood in front of him — and did not recognise him. He knew them
immediately.

When at last he told them who he was, he did not take revenge. He said:

> No blame upon you today. May Allah forgive you, and He is the most merciful
> of the merciful.
> — Sūrat Yūsuf, 12:92

He sent his shirt back to his father, whose sight returned to him, and
brought his family to Egypt. The dream of the eleven stars had taken most of
a lifetime to arrive.

## What to take from it

Every single step that looked like ruin — the well, the sale, the false
accusation, the years in prison — turned out to be part of the road to the
storehouses of Egypt. Yūsuf ﷺ could not have seen that from inside the well.
Neither can we, from inside ours.
